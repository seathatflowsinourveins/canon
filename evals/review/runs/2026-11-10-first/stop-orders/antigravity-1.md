# Review of `724246e85ed29d138cd1e5d3af7e2c3cc6d4840b` in `packages/canon-alpaca/`

1. **`packages/canon-alpaca/src/canon_alpaca/execution.py`, `AlpacaExecutionClient._denial` (lines 888–892) and `packages/canon-alpaca/README.md` (lines 197–204)**
   * **What goes wrong:** `STOP_MARKET` orders with `display_qty` set are not denied before reaching Alpaca; `display_qty` is silently ignored and the full `quantity` is submitted to Alpaca as a plain `StopOrderRequest`, contradicting both `execution.py` line 70 (*"Post-only, reduce-only, quote-quantity, display quantity, contingent orders and order lists are denied or not implemented"*) and `packages/canon-alpaca/README.md` lines 197–204 (*"So are ... display quantity orders ... Denied orders never reach Alpaca"*). When this commit added `STOP_MARKET` and `STOP_LIMIT`, it changed `if order.order_type == OrderType.LIMIT:` to `if order.order_type in LIMIT_PRICED:` (`LIMIT` and `STOP_LIMIT`), leaving `if order.display_qty is not None: return "display quantity is not supported"` inside that branch. Unlike `MarketOrder`, NautilusTrader's `StopMarketOrder` (`OrderType.STOP_MARKET`) has `display_qty: Quantity | None` (`OrderFactory.stop_market(..., display_qty=...)`).
   * **How shown:** Submitted `order_factory.stop_market(AAPL, OrderSide.BUY, Quantity.from_int(2), Price.from_str("200.00"), display_qty=Quantity.from_int(1))` from a strategy on a `LiveNode` with `AlpacaExecutionClient` and the offline stand-ins (`FakeOrdersClient`, `FakeTradeStream`, `FakeStream`). Instead of emitting `OrderDenied`, the client emitted `OrderSubmitted`, passed `StopOrderRequest(symbol='AAPL', qty=2.0, stop_price=200.0, time_in_force=TimeInForce.GTC, ...)` to `FakeOrdersClient.submit_order`, and emitted `OrderAccepted` and `OrderFilled`.

2. **`packages/canon-alpaca/src/canon_alpaca/execution.py`, `AlpacaExecutionClient._denial` (lines 879–887) and `AlpacaExecutionClient._order_request` (lines 897–915) *(unsure whether NautilusTrader intends `trigger_instrument_id` on non-emulated venue stops)***
   * **What goes wrong:** `StopMarketOrder` and `StopLimitOrder` accept `trigger_instrument_id: InstrumentId | None` (`OrderFactory.stop_market(..., trigger_instrument_id=...)` and `OrderFactory.stop_limit(..., trigger_instrument_id=...)`). When `emulation_trigger` is `None`, neither `OrderEmulator` nor `RiskEngine` intercepts or denies a stop or stop-limit order whose `trigger_instrument_id` differs from `order.instrument_id`. `_denial` does not check `order.trigger_instrument_id`, and `_order_request` ignores it and sends `StopOrderRequest` / `StopLimitOrderRequest` with `symbol=order.instrument_id.symbol.value`, which Alpaca elects on `order.instrument_id`'s prints rather than `order.trigger_instrument_id`'s prints. (I am unsure whether NautilusTrader considers `trigger_instrument_id` meaningful on non-emulated venue stop orders—`OrderStatusReport` has no `trigger_instrument_id` field—or only on `OrderEmulator` orders, but the runtime forwards such an order directly to `_submit_order` without denial.)
   * **How shown:** Submitted `order_factory.stop_market(AAPL, OrderSide.BUY, Quantity.from_int(2), Price.from_str("200.00"), trigger_instrument_id=InstrumentId.from_str("SPY.ALPACA"))` and the corresponding `stop_limit` order on a `LiveNode` with `AlpacaExecutionClient` and the offline stand-ins. Both passed `OrderEmulator`, `RiskEngine`, and `_denial`, and sent `StopOrderRequest` / `StopLimitOrderRequest` for `symbol='AAPL'` to `FakeOrdersClient.submitted`.

3. **`packages/canon-alpaca/src/canon_alpaca/execution.py`, `AlpacaExecutionClient._order_report` (lines 1157–1164)**
   * **What goes wrong:** Calling `ClientOrderId(order.client_order_id)` at line 1160 outside `order_status_report`'s `try ... except ValueError` block crashes order-status report generation—and aborts start-up reconciliation—whenever Alpaca returns a closed or `replaced` order whose `client_order_id` is rejected by `ClientOrderId` (empty `""`, whitespace-only `" "`, or containing non-ASCII characters such as `"café"`). Before this commit, `_order_report` called `order_status_report(order, self.clock.timestamp_ns())` directly, where `ClientOrderId(order.client_order_id)` ran inside `try ... except ValueError`: a closed or `replaced` order with such a `client_order_id` returned `None` (logged as a warning and skipped), and an open one raised `ValueError(f"Alpaca order {order.id} ({order.symbol}) is open with {e}")`. Hoisting `ClientOrderId(order.client_order_id)` into `_order_report` without catching `ValueError` causes any closed external order with an empty, whitespace, or non-ASCII `client_order_id` in `_generate_order_status_reports` (`QueryOrderStatus.ALL`) to raise an uncaught `ValueError`, failing mass status and start-up reconciliation (and for an open order, losing the `Alpaca order {order.id} ({order.symbol}) is open with ...` context).
   * **How shown:** Verified that `order_status_report(AlpacaOrder(**alpaca_order(client_order_id=cid, status="canceled")), 0)` returns `None` for both `cid=""` and `cid="café"`, then ran a `LiveNode` with `trading.orders = [alpaca_order(client_order_id=cid, status="canceled")]` for each: both failed start-up reconciliation with `ValueError` (`invalid string for 'value', was empty` / `invalid string for 'value' contained a non-ASCII char, was 'café'`) and aborted `node.run_async()` with `RuntimeError: Failed to get mass status from ALPACA`.

4. **`packages/canon-alpaca/tests/conftest.py`, `FakeOrdersClient.submit_order` (line 387)**
   * **What goes wrong:** `stop_price=str(getattr(order_data, "stop_price", None) or "") or None` uses truthiness (`or ""`) instead of checking `is not None`. Because `0.0` is falsy in Python, a `StopOrderRequest` or `StopLimitOrderRequest` with `stop_price=0.0` (which `alpaca-py` accepts and which reaches the client when `LiveRiskEngineConfig(bypass=True)` is used) records `stop_price=None` instead of `"0.0"` on the stand-in's stored `AlpacaOrder`, causing `order_status_report` on that order to fail with `ValueError("no price")`.
   * **How shown:** Constructed `req = StopOrderRequest(symbol="AAPL", qty=1, side=OrderSide.BUY, time_in_force=TimeInForce.DAY, stop_price=0.0)` (`req.stop_price == 0.0`); `str(getattr(req, "stop_price", None) or "") or None` evaluates to `None`.

5. **`packages/canon-alpaca/README.md`, "Known limits of the execution slice" (lines 200–201)**

6. **`packages/canon-alpaca/README.md`, "Known limits of the execution slice" (lines 202–203)**
   * **What goes wrong:** States unconditionally under **Order types**: *"An open stop is reported with its stop price as trigger price and trigger type `DEFAULT`."* In `AlpacaExecutionClient._order_report` (`execution.py` lines 1160–1164), when `self.cache` holds the order, `held.trigger_type` is passed to `order_status_report`. An open `STOP_MARKET` or `STOP_LIMIT` order submitted by the node with `TriggerType.LAST_PRICE` is therefore reported with `trigger_type=TriggerType.LAST_PRICE`, not `TriggerType.DEFAULT` (`DEFAULT` is only used when the node does not hold the order, such as at start-up reconciliation, or when the order was submitted with `DEFAULT`).
   * **How shown:** Ran a `LiveNode` with `FakeOrdersClient` (`script="accept"`), submitted `order_factory.stop_market(AAPL, OrderSide.BUY, Quantity.from_int(2), Price.from_str("200.00"), trigger_type=TriggerType.LAST_PRICE)`, and called `client._order_report(held_alpaca)` once accepted; it returned an `OrderStatusReport` with `trigger_type == TriggerType.LAST_PRICE`.
